PILLAR 1, OPERATIONAL FRAGILITY AS COMMERCIAL RISK
Type: Audience
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: This is the ICP's world, not Tony's. Their firm looks
successful, but risk is sitting in silos, unmeasured, until a resignation,
audit, or near-miss forces it into view. This pillar names the exposure
before the prospect has language for it, which is what makes the free BORe
Diagnostic feel obvious rather than sold.

Sub-topics:
1. You are one resignation away from a governance crisis, and nobody has measured it
2. The 5-point exposure check I run before I look at anything else: governance, data integrity, key-person, friction, resilience
3. Six disconnected systems cost more than one integrated one, and nobody has done that math
4. Calling your best person "irreplaceable" is an unpriced liability, not a compliment
5. The board question every leader will eventually face: how fast can you actually recover
6. A clean audit report is not proof your structure is sound, it's proof nobody has stress-tested it yet
7. Insurance pays for the damage. It does not pay back the weeks you spend not operating

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Framework/checklist text posts, the 5-point and similar exposure checks are the pillar's signature move
→ Carousel, multi-step audits (vendor dependency, shadow-IT, key-person) teach and save well as a numbered breakdown
→ Story posts, real client anecdotes (compliance breach undetected for 6 months, contractor access left live 8 months) make the risk concrete

PILLAR 2, THE ENTERPRISE ARCHITECT GAP
Type: Opinion
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: This is Tony's category claim, stated as a belief most
advisors in the space would not say publicly: nobody is reading the whole
structure, because the mid-market has never had an enterprise architect in
the room. It draws the line between Tony and every brand-led consultant,
IT vendor, or single-department fix competing for the same budget.

Sub-topics:
1. Your IT provider, your accountant and your consultant each see one slice of your business. Nobody sees the whole
2. A $100M enterprise budgets six figures for the architect role. Most $20M firms don't know the role exists
3. Stop asking your IT provider to manage your risk. That was never their job
4. The one hire enterprise firms make that mid-market firms never do
5. An advisor gives you an opinion. An architect owns the whole drawing
6. Why I don't take a cut from any vendor: an independent read only works if nobody's paying me to like the answer
7. The 4-question test that separates an architect from a vendor

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Contrarian text posts, the category-gap argument lands hardest as a direct, unhedged statement
→ Comparison carousels, advisor vs architect, vendor vs independent, sets up the point of difference visually
→ Framework posts, the 4-question and similar diagnostic tests give the opinion something concrete to stand on

PILLAR 3, RESILIENCE OVER GROWTH
Type: Methodology
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: Growth is the only number most boards currently track.
This pillar teaches the replacement metric, recovery time, and gives it a
named system (S-Architecture: Scale, Secure, Sustain, Shield, Succession)
so the audience has language to bring the idea back into their own
boardroom, which is what turns a reader into a referral source.

Sub-topics:
1. Stop measuring growth. Start measuring recovery time
2. Scale, Secure, Sustain, Shield, Succession, the 5 pillars of a firm built to outlast its founder
3. Growth is not the safety net most leaders think it is. Sometimes it hides the crack
4. Fast-growing and resilient are not the same business, and most board packs never tell you which one you're looking at
5. Eighteen months of zero unplanned downtime is not luck. It is structure
6. Succession is the only pillar that finishes with the founder no longer required. That's the actual goal
7. The 4-line resilience benchmark every board should track alongside revenue

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Proof/recap posts, the 18-month zero-downtime and S-Architecture pillar recaps carry Tony's strongest numbers
→ Framework carousels, the recovery-time budget and resilience benchmark teach a repeatable board-ready model
→ Story posts, the two founder-origin posts (IBM redundancy) belong here as the human proof behind the method
