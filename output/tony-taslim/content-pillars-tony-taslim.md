PILLAR 1, Operational Fragility as Commercial Risk
Type: Audience-centric
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: This is the ICP's own unmeasured exposure, told back to them in their
language. A COO or CFO of a $10M-$100M firm feels the fragility (the near-miss, the
resignation that nearly broke month-end) before they have a name for it. This pillar names
the five risk areas (governance, data integrity, key-person dependency, operational
friction, resilience) as one connected structure, and reframes each as a priced commercial
risk rather than an operational annoyance.

Sub-topics:
1. The one-resignation test: what happens to month-end, payroll, or client delivery if
   your best-documented person leaves this Friday
2. Why "we've never had an incident" is proof you haven't been tested, not proof you're safe
3. The friction tax: what six disconnected systems cost that one integrated one would not
4. Calling someone "irreplaceable" is an unpriced liability, not a compliment
5. The 48-hour absence test for real key-person dependency, run before any resilience claim
6. Why a clean audit report only proves nobody has stress-tested the structure yet
7. Pricing a shock in hours, not feelings: the framework for what a bad week actually costs

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Framework post, gives the leader a self-run test they can apply before their next board
  meeting
→ Client story (anonymised), makes the risk concrete instead of abstract
→ Contrarian take, reframes a comfort signal (no incidents, a fast "yes," a clean audit)
  as a blind spot

PILLAR 2, The Enterprise Architect Gap
Type: Opinion / category-defining
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: This is Tony's category claim, the argument that a $100M enterprise reads
its whole structure across people, process, technology and risk before every board meeting,
and a $20-30M firm has never had anyone read it once. It draws the line between an advisor
who owns one department's opinion and an architect who owns the whole drawing, and it is
where the Alcatel-Lucent and IBM pedigree earns its place without becoming a résumé post.

Sub-topics:
1. What a $100M enterprise gets that a $20M firm never does: an architect in the room
2. Your IT provider, your accountant and your consultant each see one slice. Nobody sees
   the whole
3. The 3-question test that separates an architect from a vendor
4. Why the cheapest fix up front is rarely the cheapest fix overall, it just moves the bill
   to the next crisis
5. What the same 3-layer read (people, process, technology) looks like run on a $100M
   telecom system versus a $15M firm's Monday meeting
6. Independence as the actual differentiator: why an advisor paid the same regardless of
   vendor has no reason to steer you wrong
7. The difference between a project manager who keeps you on schedule and an architect who
   makes sure the finished project doesn't cause the next five problems

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Contrarian take, challenges the leader's current advisor stack directly
→ Framework post, gives a test the leader can run on their own consultants and vendors
→ Soft-sell, ties the category claim to the free Business Resilience Review or Executive
  Breakthrough Session

PILLAR 3, Resilience Over Growth
Type: Methodology-led
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: This is Tony's replacement metric for the board pack: growth used to be
the only number that mattered, and recovery time is becoming the other one every board
will ask for. It carries the S-Architecture framework (Scale, Secure, Sustain, Shield,
Succession) as the methodology arc, from fragile success through durable control to a firm
that outlives its founder.

Sub-topics:
1. Stop measuring growth. Start measuring recovery time
2. The one board question every leader will face: how fast can you recover
3. Scale, Secure, Sustain, Shield, Succession: what each S-Architecture pillar actually
   tests, one post per pillar
4. Why a fast-growing business and a resilient business are not the same business, and
   most board packs never tell you which one you're looking at
5. The 3-part recovery clock: detect, decide, restore, priced separately, before a shock
  not after one
6. Insurance pays for the damage. It does not pay back the weeks you spent not operating
7. Succession is the only S-Architecture pillar that finishes with the founder no longer
   required, and why that is the actual goal

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Framework post, the recurring self-score or recovery-clock tools readers can save and
  reuse
→ Client story, an 18-months-zero-downtime style proof point translated into a board number
→ Recap post, walks one S-Architecture pillar at a time across a series
