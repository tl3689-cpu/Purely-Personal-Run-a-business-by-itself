PILLAR 1, OPERATIONAL FRAGILITY AS COMMERCIAL RISK
Type: Audience
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: The ICP's own world. A COO, CFO or CEO of a founder-led
$10M-$100M firm carries five compounding risks (governance, data integrity,
key-person, friction, resilience) that nobody has read as one structure.
This pillar names the fragility in the reader's language before Tony
appears as the answer, and frames it as a structure gap, never a people gap.

Sub-topics:
1. The pallet pooler whose return scanner was switched off by a software update, leaving eleven weeks of hire uninvoiced
2. The industrial gas distributor whose test-due report filtered on "active" while cylinders on hire were marked "on loan"
3. Count the retypes: how many times the same figure is typed by hand between quote and invoice
4. The exit-note read: underline any word that appears twice in the last five exit notes
5. Why an approval chain with six signatures is one decision with six people hoping the others read it
6. The sticky-note census: each note on a desk is a procedure the system forgot to keep
7. Why closing the month a day faster every year is not improvement if the steps never changed

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Pain-point text posts naming the exposure in the reader's own words
→ Numbered carousels walking the 5-risk lens as one system
→ "Are you doing this?" posts that trigger DMs and self-recognition

PILLAR 2, THE ENTERPRISE ARCHITECT GAP
Type: Opinion
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: The category claim. Nobody serves the mid-market with a
360-degree structural read across people, process, technology and risk,
chosen on fit and independent of brand. This pillar draws the line between
what a $100M enterprise gets (an architect reading the structure on a
schedule) and what a $20M-$40M firm has only been offered (a vendor, an IT
provider, a single-department consultant). Opinionated by design.

Sub-topics:
1. The two-column inventory: every system on the left, the person who decides when it changes on the right
2. Why best of breed in every department can add up to worst of breed at the borders between them
3. Why an IT strategy only the IT team has read is a technology plan, not a business plan
4. A preferred supplier list is a list of relationships, not a list of fits
5. The end-of-support letter: ask each vendor in writing, and sort your technology into planned and accidental
6. The tow fleet whose dispatch map kept the old suburb boundary, and the $210,000 penalty that followed
7. The court reporting company whose certified transcript carried uncorrected draft pages from three versions in email

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Contrarian text posts, plain and unhedged
→ Comparison carousels, $20M-$40M firm vs $100M enterprise on the same structural questions
→ Proof/story posts grounded in scenarios, translated into a mid-market lesson

PILLAR 3, RESILIENCE OVER GROWTH
Type: Methodology
─────────────────────────────────────────
Why this pillar: Tony's system made concrete. Growth was the only number a
board wanted, and recovery time is becoming the other. This pillar carries
the S-Architecture framework (Scale, Secure, Sustain, Shield, Succession)
and THRIVE, translating "resilience" into board-ready numbers a CFO can
read. It pre-sells the Business Resilience Review, Executive Breakthrough
Session, THRIVE, R.I.V.E and adjacent offers without a hard close. The
Fractional Resilience Advisor retainer is rested in the 766-825 batch (last
used at row 700) to hold the ~130-row distance standard.

Sub-topics:
1. The superannuation administrator whose custodian changed one file format overnight and held 18,000 statements for six days
2. There is no such thing as an average week, so plan for the weeks that actually arrive
3. The busiest-days map: the first thing that breaks at 30 percent over plan
4. The three-sentence recovery plan: who calls whom, what customers are told, what happens first
5. Why a 99 percent on-time rate is decided in the other 1 percent
6. Why stretching supplier payment terms is borrowing your supplier's resilience
7. The wool broker whose buyer credit limits stayed behind in the retired system, $1.4 million over in one sale day

Best formats for this pillar:
→ Step-by-step carousels, the three-sentence recovery plan and the S-Architecture self-score
→ Framework text posts, the recovery-time reframe as a board metric
→ Case/proof posts translated into board language
